Agent Service Index

Live Founder · May 2026 –

The discovery layer for the agent economy.

agentserviceindex.com · Solo founder · MCP & x402 on Base

AI agents can't really transact yet. Every signup is a captcha, an OAuth dance, a dashboard built for a human with a credit card. ASI is the missing piece: a public registry where agents send a natural-language query ("cloud hosting," "a subdomain on the open internet," "a way to pay another agent in USDC") and get back services they can actually call. Onboarding is one prompt. No keys to rotate, no plans to pick.

The registry ships with three first-party services that prove the stack end-to-end — permissionless EC2, instant subdomains via Cloudflare Tunnel, and an x402 payment facilitator that moves USDC on Base. Built on MCP, EIP-3009, and the assumption that within a year the median internet customer will not have hands.

  • cloud-as Permissionless EC2 instance management with free and paid tiers.
  • subdomain & CDN Automated DNS binding and Cloudflare Tunnel provisioning — agents put a site on the open internet without ever touching a console.
  • x402 facilitator USDC transfers on Base, EIP-3009 compliant, v2 of the x402 protocol.

Sidequest Local

Live Founder · Dec 2025 –

For when your friends are busy, but you're not.

sidequestlocal.com · Sidequest Local, Inc. · Charlottesville, VA

You have a free afternoon. You text ten group chats. Six don't reply, three are busy, one says "maybe later." You stay home. Sidequest is the app that replaces that whole loop with a feed.

It's a mobile social-discovery app for spontaneous local meetups. Open it and you see what small groups of people near you are doing right now — coffee, climbing, board games, a walk to the bookstore — and you tap once to join. Posting a plan takes about thirty seconds. No group-chat coordination, no RSVPs, no calendar invites. The whole product is built around lowering the activation energy of leaving the house.

Counterprogramming for an agent-and-feed-dominated internet: ASI is for software that doesn't have hands, Sidequest is for humans who forgot they did.

Flexible Input Networks (FINs)

Jun 2024 – 2025

Air Force Research Laboratory · QC Ware Corp. · USAFA · Individual researcher

The Air Force has a problem called the Airlift Logistics Challenge: a Pickup-and-Delivery instance where the number of aircraft, cargo items, and airfields all change between problem instances, and the policy has to output a variable number of actions at each step. No standard neural architecture handles that gracefully — transformers want fixed input shapes, RNNs want a sequence, GNNs want a stable graph.

So I designed Flexible Input Networks: a class of architectures that learn over "sets of sets" of arbitrary cardinality and output variable-length action sets in a single forward pass — no autoregression, no recurrence, no padding tricks. FINs gave the Airlift Challenge its first reinforcement-learning solution. The formalism generalizes beyond logistics to any multi-modal, variable-cardinality problem.

#1 USAFA Computer Science Research Project · Summer 2024 Briefed at CORONA 2024 — to the Secretary of the Air Force and Chief of Staff of the Air Force.

Informed Machine Learning

May – Aug 2023

NSA Mathematics Research Group · USAFA · Team of 4

A summer at the NSA's math group, trying to answer: can you make an LLM hallucinate less on technical text by bolting an expert-curated ontology into its encoder, rather than by making it bigger? We built an attention-based autoencoder whose encoder was fused with a domain knowledge base, then evaluated it on semantic extraction over scientifically nuanced documents.

The informed model beat the un-informed baseline on extraction quality, evidence that "informed" architectures may scale accuracy more sample-efficiently than the raw-parameter-count playbook. Most of the project was the bridge between symbolic knowledge and attention layers — figuring out where the ontology gets to live inside a transformer without breaking pretraining.

#1 USAFA Junior Engineering Research Project · Summer 2023 (CSRP, Engineering Division) Briefed to the Director of the NSA; presented at CORONA 2023 to the Secretary of the Air Force and Chief of Staff of the Air Force.

NATO Alliance Space Deterrence

Aug 2022 – May 2023

USAFA · NATO Science & Technology Organization · Led team of 29

Russia destroyed a satellite with a kinetic ASAT in late 2021 and left a debris field that's still in orbit. NATO commissioned a deterrence framework spanning the diplomatic, informational, military, and economic instruments of power. I led the 29-cadet research team that built it.

The output was a set of policy frameworks for national and alliance authorities — how the alliance should signal, escalate, and respond to attacks in a domain where attribution is hard, collateral damage is permanent, and there are no rules of the road. Less a paper and more an attempt to write the first draft of a body of doctrine that doesn't yet exist.

Briefed at NATO's inaugural space-deterrence wargame in Paris, with thirteen member nations represented.

I built malware that phishes your friends

Aug 2024 – May 2025

USAFA Center for Cyberspace Research · Team of 3 · Proof of concept

Listed in the academic catalog as "Automated Conversational Hijacking." What it actually is: agentic malware that gets into your email account, reads your existing threads, and replies inside those threads with spear-phishing messages that sound like you wrote them. Same writing voice, same inside jokes, threading into a real conversation already in your inbox — so the target opens it.

The point wasn't to ship an attack. The point was to show what the social-engineering threat surface looks like the moment LLMs become competent at reading context and writing convincingly. The result is uncomfortably good. The defensive posture most organizations have today — "users will spot phishing if it sounds off" — is now a bet against a model that has read your last six months of email.

EEG Brain–Computer Interface (P300 Speller)

Aug 2023 – Feb 2024

USAFA Center for Cyberspace Research · Individual · Full stack: hardware → classifier

A P300 speller is a system that lets you type by thinking. Letters flash on a grid; when the one you're attending to flashes, your brain produces a measurable spike about 300ms later. A good classifier turns those spikes into characters.

I built the whole pipeline end-to-end — the EEG helmet hardware, the flashing stimulus GUI, the data collection harness, and a recurrent CNN classifier that translates raw scalp voltages into typed text. The primary use case is communication for people with ALS or other severe motor disabilities, who have intact cognition but no reliable way to output language. Soldering up the helmet taught me more about signal noise than any class did.

Won a rover race against other cadets

Jan – Mar 2024

USAFA Center for Cyberspace Research · Individual · Behavioral cloning → actor-critic RL

The class assignment: train a vision-only autonomous rover, then race it head-to-head against the other students' rovers on a track none of us had seen. No LIDAR, no GPS, no map — just a camera and whatever policy you could train in time.

I started with behavioral cloning from a handful of human-driven laps to get a working baseline, then fine-tuned with an actor-critic RL setup and a lot of reward engineering — penalizing the things that ended runs (clipping cones, oversteer on tight turns) and rewarding clean lines. The RL policy beat the cloning-only baseline by a wide margin and beat the other cadets' rovers on race day. The fun engineering problem turned out to be the reward function, not the architecture.

Facial Recognition Similarity Query

Mar – May 2023

USAFA · Rose-Hulman Institute of Technology · Team of 4 · Research only

Show the system one photo of a person, and it pulls every other photo of that person out of a 40,000+ sports-photo database. The backbone is a CNN transfer-learned from a face-recognition base model and fine-tuned with a triplet loss — so embeddings of the same person cluster together, and different people drift apart. Queries hit a vector database with cosine similarity over the learned embedding space.

A nice toy version of a fundamentally serious problem. We kept it inside the lab — useful as a study of embedding quality and triplet-mining strategies, not as something to deploy on actual humans without their consent.
